    READING THE CALCULATOR

    A sensitivity map, step by step.

    01

    Start with the data

    The selected future, exact option expiration, each strike’s IV and the source’s position assumptions determine what can be calculated. Delayed and example snapshots are clearly labeled. Levels converted from an index or ETF need a disclosed conversion; they are proxies rather than native futures-option strikes.

    02

    Compare Gamma with its modeled peak

    100% means near the strongest price sensitivity for that option at the same IV and remaining time. Falling toward 10% means Delta changes less for another price move. It does not mean selling volume fell by 90%.

    03

    Read the two fading boundaries

    Each percentage has an upper and lower price boundary. Gamma can fade on either side of the sensitive region. These are modeled distances, not support, resistance or trading signals.

    04

    Separate exposures from actual inventory

    A negative GEX bar alone does not reveal the number of dealer contracts. Morning-plan scenarios use 100 assumed contracts. Market-watch scenarios use the selected open-interest assumption. Actual market impact depends on inventory, liquidity and other flows.

    Model scope

    Price scenarios hold IV and time unchanged. The futures-option model is an approximation and may differ from the provider’s Greeks or the exchange’s exercise-style model. Missing IV or expiration disables sensitivity estimates.
